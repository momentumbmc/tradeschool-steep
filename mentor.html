Practitioner Journey

From research analyst to professional trader, and why he teaches

[insert mentor first name] began his 16-year market career as a stock market research analyst, studying businesses, balance sheets, and market structure from the inside. Over time, he moved from research into professional trading, spending years testing observations across shifting market cycles and developing his own disciplined methods rooted in price action, liquidity, and institutional order flow.

Across Kerala and India, he kept watching capable people from every background, including students, engineers, doctors, and business owners, lose confidence and hard-earned savings after being taught mechanical indicators and crowded batch rules. When market conditions changed, those rules broke, and nobody could explain why.

That frustration turned into a clear mission: bringing genuine, mechanics-first stock market education to deserving students in Kerala and across India. He does not want to build a giant community of lakhs of students. Instead, he chooses to work quietly with a small number of committed learners who actually practice real stock market learning and learn to think for themselves.

Methodology & Classroom Posture

How your mentor reads markets and teaches you

In his own market work, [insert mentor first name] starts on the higher timeframe (monthly, weekly, and daily context), identifies where large participants are likely to find liquidity, and brings that read down to the lower timeframe for disciplined execution. Very little depends on predicting the future. Almost all of it depends on recognizing what large participants are doing and reasoning honestly about what that implies.

In your live sessions, that discipline shows up in three ways:

01 · Plain English first

Zero intimidation for beginners

Every concept starts with an everyday analogy (like a fish market or a shopkeeper's stock) before market terminology enters the room, so complete beginners never feel shut out.

02 · Direct, respectful challenge

Catching hidden blind spots live

When you explain a chart out loud, he listens for hidden assumptions, confirmation bias, or loose risk math and questions your logic calmly until your reasoning holds up.

03 · True independence

Needing your mentor less over time

His explicit measure of success is that you need him less on day one hundred than you did on day one.
